Connecting your storage

To add a connection, open the Connections tab, tap + and choose the service. Some services open their own sign-in page; for the others you type in a key or a password. Tap Test to check it, then Save. Your passwords and keys are stored encrypted on your phone and are only sent to the service itself.

Buckets: Amazon S3 · Cloudflare R2 · Wasabi · Backblaze B2 · S3-compatible

Services you sign in to

These open the service’s own sign-in page in your browser. OrbitFiles never sees your password; the service gives it permission to your files, which you can remove at any time in your account’s settings.

Google Drive

  1. Choose Google Drive and tap Sign in.
  2. Pick your Google account and allow access to your files.

If Google says it hasn’t verified the app yet, tap Advanced and continue: Google is still reviewing OrbitFiles. Google Docs, Sheets and Slides are copied as Word, Excel and PowerPoint files.

Dropbox

  1. Choose Dropbox and tap Sign in.
  2. Sign in and tap Allow.

OneDrive

  1. Choose OneDrive and tap Sign in.
  2. Sign in with your Microsoft account and accept.

Work and school accounts may need their administrator to approve OrbitFiles first.

pCloud

  1. Choose pCloud and tap Sign in.
  2. Sign in and allow access. OrbitFiles finds out whether your account is in the US or EU region by itself.

Mega

  1. Choose Mega.
  2. Enter the email and password you sign in to mega.nz with.

Mega doesn’t offer a separate sign-in page, so this one is typed in. It stays on your phone.

Bucket storage (S3 and similar)

These use an access key: a pair of values made in the service’s website, so you don’t give OrbitFiles your password. The secret part is shown only once, so copy it straight away. Leave Bucket empty to see all your buckets, or enter one bucket’s name to open just that one.

Amazon S3

  1. In the AWS console, open IAM › Users, choose or create a user, and give it access to S3 (for example the AmazonS3FullAccess policy, or a policy for one bucket).
  2. Open the user’s Security credentials tab and choose Create access key.
  3. In OrbitFiles choose Amazon S3, paste the Access key ID and Secret access key, and pick the Region your buckets are in.

If the key may only use one bucket, enter that bucket’s name: it can’t list the others.

Cloudflare R2

  1. In the Cloudflare dashboard open R2. If you don’t have a bucket yet, choose Create bucket.
  2. Choose Manage API tokens › Create Account API token. Give it Object Read & Write for your bucket (or all buckets) and create it.
  3. Copy the Access Key ID, the Secret Access Key and the S3 endpoint (https://<account-id>.r2.cloudflarestorage.com). You don’t need the “Token value”.
  4. In OrbitFiles choose Cloudflare R2, paste the three values, and type your bucket name.

An Object Read & Write token can’t list buckets, so the bucket name is needed. The endpoint is the address without the bucket name on the end. Buckets in a jurisdiction (such as EU) have their own endpoint: copy it from the bucket’s Settings page.

Wasabi

  1. In the Wasabi console open Access Keys and choose Create new access key.
  2. In OrbitFiles choose Wasabi, paste the access key and secret key, and pick the Region your buckets are in.

Backblaze B2

  1. On backblaze.com open Application Keys and choose Add a New Application Key (Read and Write).
  2. In OrbitFiles choose Backblaze B2 and paste the keyID into Key ID and the applicationKey into Application key.

The Master Application Key works too, but a separate key is safer: you can delete it without affecting anything else.

S3-compatible (MinIO and others)

  1. Make an access key in your server or provider (in MinIO: Access Keys › Create access key).
  2. In OrbitFiles choose S3-compatible, paste the keys, and enter the Endpoint URL your provider gives, for example https://minio.example.com:9000.

Your own servers

For a server at home, your phone needs to reach it: on the same Wi-Fi, or over a VPN such as Tailscale when you’re out. Use the server’s name or IP address as the Host.

SFTP (SSH)

  1. Choose SFTP and enter the Host, Port (usually 22) and Username.
  2. Enter your password, or tap Use a key file instead and choose your private key (for example id_ed25519, not the .pub one). If the key has a passphrase, type it in Key passphrase.
  3. The first time, OrbitFiles shows the server’s key fingerprint and asks whether to trust it. If you can, compare it with the server’s own (run ssh-keygen -lf /etc/ssh/ssh_host_ed25519_key.pub on it), then tap Trust.

To get a key file onto your phone, copy it into Downloads (by USB, or from your computer) and delete it from there once the connection is saved: OrbitFiles keeps its own encrypted copy. PuTTY .ppk keys need converting first: in PuTTYgen, use Conversions › Export OpenSSH key. If OrbitFiles later says the server’s key has changed and you didn’t change the server, don’t continue: someone may be in between.

FTP

  1. Choose FTP and enter the Host, Port (usually 21), Username and Password.
  2. Optionally, set a Starting folder such as /files.

Plain FTP doesn’t encrypt your password or files. Use SFTP when the server offers it.

SMB (Windows sharing, NAS, Samba)

  1. Choose SMB and enter the computer or NAS as the Host (for example 192.168.1.20).
  2. Enter the Username and Password you use for the share. Leave Domain empty unless your network uses one.
  3. Leave Starting folder empty to see all the shares, or enter one, such as /photos.

On Windows, the username is your Windows account (for a Microsoft account, its email address) and the share must be turned on in the folder’s Properties › Sharing.

WebDAV (Nextcloud, ownCloud and others)

  1. Find your server’s WebDAV address. In Nextcloud it’s under Files › Files settings (bottom left), and looks like https://cloud.example.com/remote.php/dav/files/USERNAME/.
  2. Choose WebDAV, paste the address into URL, and enter your Username and Password.

If your account uses two-step login, make an app password (Nextcloud: Personal settings › Security › Create new app password) and use that instead of your normal password.

If it doesn’t connect

  • “Couldn’t find the server”: check the host or endpoint for typos, and that your phone is online. For a home server, check you’re on the same Wi-Fi or VPN.
  • “The server didn’t answer” or “refused the connection”: check the port, and that the server is on and allows connections from your network.
  • “The keys were refused”: paste the access key and secret again; a missing character or space is the usual cause. If you no longer have the secret, make a new key.
  • “These keys can’t list buckets”: enter the bucket name in Bucket.
  • “There’s no bucket with that name here”: bucket names are lowercase and must match exactly; also check the endpoint or region.
  • “Sign in again”: the service no longer accepts the sign-in (for example after you changed your password). Open the connection’s menu, choose Edit, and sign in again.
